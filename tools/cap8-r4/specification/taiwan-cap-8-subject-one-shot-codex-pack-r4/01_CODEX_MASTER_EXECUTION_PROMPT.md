- detect omitted explanations or prerequisites;
- compare teaching sequence and terminology;

- improve zero-foundation pedagogy;
- create an edition-reference coverage matrix.

Do not require access to proprietary full books in order to proceed. Lack of access to unlicensed full text is not a scope blocker. Do not copy protected prose, questions, illustrations, maps, or answer keys. Create independent explanations, original examples, and original parallel questions.

### 4.4 Source precedence for factual claims

1. current official curriculum/CAP source;
2. responsible government agency, official law database, official statistical/geospatial/scientific source;
3. institutionally reviewed or peer-reviewed source;
4. current approved publisher reference, only where it does not conflict with higher authority;
5. reputable secondary source for omission discovery only.

Every factual claim, curriculum mapping, and unstable date-dependent statement requires traceable evidence. Conflicts require a written resolution record.

## 9. Lecture requirements

Every atomic-skill lecture must satisfy `policies/LECTURE_STANDARD.md` and include:

- prerequisite diagnosis and zero-foundation bridge;
- precise objective linked to official codes;
- plain-language explanation before formal terminology;
- conditions, boundaries, counterexamples, and common confusions;
- multiple representations appropriate to the subject;
- at least two fully worked examples, with more where complexity demands;
- explicit reasoning steps rather than answer-only shortcuts;
- common errors and why they fail;
- retrieval, application, and transfer checks;
- remediation links and next-skill prerequisites;

A lecture cannot pass because it merely lists definitions, dates, formulas, vocabulary, or exam tricks.
