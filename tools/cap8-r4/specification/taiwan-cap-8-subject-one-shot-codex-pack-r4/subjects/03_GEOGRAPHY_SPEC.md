# Geography complete-production specification

## Construct
Geography is a subdiscipline of the combined Social exam. Teach the entire geography scope and also prepare cross-domain data reading with History and Civics. Maps, spatial scale, graphs, statistics, photos, GIS-like layers, and environmental/public issues are core representations.

## Minimum production floor
- Atomic skills and full lectures: 240
- Static skill questions: 2880 (minimum 12 per skill)
- Additional stimulus-based questions: 720
- Counts are floors, not ceilings.

2. Direction, coordinates and location
3. Scale, distance and map measurement
4. Contour lines and terrain interpretation

7. Earth surface processes and landforms
8. Weather elements and climate graphs
9. Climate controls and climate classification
10. Hydrology, watersheds and water resources
11. Population distribution, structure and migration
12. Settlement, urbanization and urban systems
13. Culture, ethnicity and human landscape
14. Agriculture, fisheries and food systems
15. Industry, location and production networks
16. Services, trade, transport and tourism
17. Development indicators and regional inequality
18. Taiwan location and physical environment
19. Taiwan climate, water and hazards
20. Taiwan population and settlement

22. Taiwan environmental issues and sustainability
23. China and East Asia
24. Southeast Asia

## Required workflow
1. Parse all applicable official curriculum performance/content codes and appendices.
2. Audit current approved textbook editions and lawful samples/errata.
3. Re-read and classify every applicable 106–115 official item.
4. Split topic families into atomic teachable skills; do not combine unrelated concepts.
5. Author source-of-truth lectures and questions.
6. Build required original figures/maps/audio/data sets.
7. Complete authoring QA, freeze the artifact, then complete the single final exhaustive semantic audit at the exact final hash.
8. Build and test subject runtime and migrate existing progress.
9. Pass subject gate before activating the next subject.
