# Complete lecture standard

Every atomic-skill lecture must contain:

1. Stable ID, subject, unit, skill, prerequisites, curriculum codes, textbook-union links, and official-exam links.
2. Zero-foundation opening: vocabulary, symbols, dates, places, people, prior concepts, or laboratory conventions needed to begin.
3. Precise learning objective and mastery criteria.
4. Concept explanation in plain Traditional Chinese; English learner content uses controlled English plus Traditional Chinese support as specified.
5. At least two representations when relevant: prose, formula, table, graph, map, timeline, diagram, source excerpt, experiment, or worked classification.
6. Boundary conditions, exceptions, and what the rule does not mean.
7. At least three worked examples spanning basic, standard CAP, and transfer demand; language subjects may use more examples rather than numerical derivation.
8. A reproducible solving/reading/inquiry procedure with checkpoints.
9. At least four distinct misconceptions or failure modes with diagnostic cues and corrections.
10. Connections to prerequisite and later skills.
11. Retrieval check, guided practice, independent practice, and transfer check.
12. Complete explanations, not answer-only feedback.
13. Source/provenance notes and factual date/version where necessary.
14. Accessibility-complete figures/audio and printable fallback.
15. Two semantic review records tied to the exact artifact hash.

Minimum length is not a substitute for quality. Conversely, excessive prose that does not improve mastery must be edited.
